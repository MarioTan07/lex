The kampung

Where Surabaya's water clover comes from

Semanggi (Marsilea crenata) is a small water fern whose four leaflets look like clover. It grows in flooded rice paddies, and for generations the families of Kendung in Kelurahan Sememi have turned it into pecel semanggi Suroboyo.

The trade started with a handful of vendors who went looking for semanggi in fields as far away as Lamongan, Mojokerto and Pasuruan. Today many families grow it themselves, so the leaves on your pincuk were often picked a few streets from where they were cooked.

Sellers used to carry the dish through Surabaya's streets in a basket slung on their backs. The kampung now runs its own delivery, and this site lets you order from the stalls directly.

What comes on a pincuk

One portion of pecel semanggi

  • LeavesSemanggi, steamed until soft, with a handful of bean sprouts.
  • SauceThick and sweet, ground from sweet potato and peanuts.
  • CrackerKrupuk puli, a crisp rice cracker for scooping.
  • PlateA pincuk: banana leaf folded into a cone and pinned.

Visit: Jalan Kendung, Kelurahan Sememi, Kecamatan Benowo, Surabaya.

History

From street baskets to heritage

  1. 1950s

    Pecel semanggi is already sold around Surabaya by vendors from Kendung.

  2. 2017

    Kampoeng Semanggi opens officially in Sememi.

  3. 2021

    Joins Kampung Berseri Astra, which backs new semanggi products.

  4. 2022

    Pecel Semanggi Suroboyo is listed as national intangible cultural heritage.

  5. 2025

    The kampung's delivery service wins an East Java regional innovation award.
